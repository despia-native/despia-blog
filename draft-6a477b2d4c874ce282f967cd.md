---
title: "Fix Guideline 3.1.2(c) Subscription Rejection on iOS"

---

You shipped an app with an auto-renewable subscription, and Apple bounced it with Guideline 3.1.2(c). This is one of the most common rejections for subscription apps, and one of the most mechanical to fix. It is almost never about your purchase code. It is about four pieces of information Apple needs to see, in two places, before it approves the app.

The rejection you got has two parts. They look similar but they are separate problems, and you have to fix both.

## Part one: the required information is missing

Apple requires every app with an auto-renewable subscription to show, inside the app, all of the following:

*   The title of the subscription
    
*   The length of the subscription
    
*   The price, and the price per unit where it applies
    
*   Functional links to your privacy policy and your Terms of Use (EULA)
    

If any of these is missing from the screen where the user subscribes, you get the first 3.1.2(c) hit. Missing links are the most common cause, followed by a price that is shown but not tied to a duration.

In Despia your paywall is your web UI, so all of this lives in your own code. There is no native screen to rebuild. You add the required block to the paywall you already have and ship it.

```html
<div class="sub-terms">
  <p id="sub-line">Premium Monthly. 1 month. $9.99/month, auto-renews until cancelled.</p>
  <a href="/terms">Terms of Use</a>
  <a href="/privacy">Privacy Policy</a>
</div>
```

Two things people get wrong here.

First, do not hardcode the price. Apple charges the store-localized price, and if the number on your paywall does not match what StoreKit actually charges (wrong currency, wrong amount in another region) you can get rejected again. Pull the price string from the product so the two always agree.

```javascript
// Show the store-localized price so the paywall matches what StoreKit charges
const pkg = offerings.current.availablePackages.find(p => p.identifier === 'monthly')
document.getElementById('sub-line').textContent =
  `Premium Monthly. 1 month. ${pkg.product.priceString}/month, auto-renews until cancelled.`
```

Second, the links have to be functional. Apple tests them. A dead link, a link to a page that does not load, or a link that navigates the WebView to a page the user cannot back out of all count as failures. The clean pattern is to serve your legal pages as real routes inside your own app (`/terms`, `/privacy`) so the user stays in the app and can navigate back, and to make sure both pages are live before you resubmit.

## Part two: the app metadata is missing the links too

The in-app links are not enough on their own. App Store Connect has its own fields, and Apple checks them separately:

*   The privacy policy URL goes in the **Privacy Policy** field in App Store Connect.
    
*   The Terms of Use (EULA) goes either in the **App Description** or in the dedicated **EULA** field.
    

If you are using Apple's standard Terms of Use, add the link to your App Description. Apple's standard EULA lives at `apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/stdeula`. If you wrote your own EULA, put it in the custom license agreement field in App Store Connect instead.

This is metadata, not code, so it does not go through a build. You set it in App Store Connect and it applies on your next submission.

## Part three: describe what the user actually gets

The second rejection in your message is a different failure. Apple is saying the subscription does not clearly describe what the user receives for the price. This is a content problem, not a links problem.

A paywall that says "Subscribe for $9.99/month" and nothing else will get this hit. Apple wants the user to know what they are paying for before they pay. State the benefits in plain terms on the paywall itself:

```html
<ul class="sub-benefits">
  <li>Unlimited projects</li>
  <li>Offline sync across devices</li>
  <li>Priority support</li>
</ul>
```

Keep it specific to your app. "Premium features" is not a description. "Unlimited exports, no watermark, cloud backup" is.

## Why this is faster to fix in Despia

Your paywall is web content, and Despia ships web content over the air. You fix the terms block, the localized price, and the benefits list in your codebase, push the update, and the reviewer sees the corrected paywall in the version they are testing. There is no second native project to touch and no separate submission for the in-app change. You update App Store Connect for the metadata fields, push the paywall fix, and resubmit the same binary.

If you want to confirm the reviewer will see the change, the environment check is the standard one:

```javascript
const isDespia = navigator.userAgent.toLowerCase().includes('despia')
if (isDespia) despia('successhaptic://')  // fires only inside the native app
```

## What to send back to Apple

Once the paywall shows the title, length, localized price, and both functional links, and the App Store Connect fields are set, reply to the reviewer in Resolution Center with a short screen recording that walks through the paywall and taps both legal links so they open. Add the same detail to the Notes field in App Review Information so your next submission does not hit this again.
